prerelation-js — the prerelation coefficient, in your browser

What should be learned first?

A coefficient for prerequisite relations between skills reported on a common anchored scale — with a calculator that runs entirely in this page, on your own data.

1Your question

Anyone who designs instruction, curricula, learning apps, or diagnostic assessments makes ordering decisions all the time: what to teach first, which skill unlocks the next, where a learning pathway should branch. These decisions usually rest on expert judgment and curricular tradition. Data could inform them — but the standard statistical toolbox was built to measure association, not order.

The familiar correlation answers a symmetric question: do X and Y move together? Prerequisite-ness is asymmetric: does progress in Y require X first? A high correlation cannot tell X→Y from Y→X, nor from “both reflect one shared ability” — and two nearly identical skills correlate perfectly while neither is a prerequisite of the other. When scores live on a common anchored scale (0 = ignorance, 1 = mastery), a true prerequisite relation leaves a specific one-sided footprint in the data that a symmetric coefficient cannot express.

The prerelation coefficient Π scores exactly that footprint for an ordered pair. Reach for it when your scores are anchored on [0, 1] and your question is about ordering — sequencing a curriculum, checking a hypothesized skill hierarchy against data, or screening skill pairs before building an adaptive pathway. It complements correlation rather than replacing it: a different question, not a better answer.

2What Π and Δ tell you

Δ — the prerelation direction coefficient

Δ = Π(X→Y) − Π(Y→X), on [−1, +1] and antisymmetric. The sign is the direction — positive means X is the prerequisite side — and the magnitude is the strength of the asymmetry. It reads like a signed coefficient, the natural counterpart to how practitioners read r.

Read Δ together with the Π pair. Δ = 0 by itself does not distinguish “no relation” from “equivalent skills” — both put the two directions on an equal footing.

Π — per-direction strength

Π lives on [0, 1]: 0 means no prerequisite relation, 1 means a perfect prerequisite relation. It is a continuous quantity, read like a correlation magnitude — there are no thresholds and no cutoffs anywhere on this page.

The reading ladder

First the permutation p — is there a relation at all? Then Δ — which direction, and how asymmetric? Then the Π pair — the per-direction strength.

Notation: values computed from data are shown as plain Π and Δ, estimated from your data. Correlation is shown as Pearson r, a sample statistic (its population counterpart is written ρ).

3Side-by-side with correlation

Four simulated skill pairs, one per panel — generated in this browser by the shipped library and scored live. Each panel shows Pearson r, Δ, Π(X→Y) and Π(Y→X), estimated from the sample drawn.

The equivalence panel is the centerpiece: r is close to 1 while Δ = 0 and both Π = 0. That is a mathematical property of the two definitions. Read one way: r registers the co-movement of two nearly identical skills. Read the other way: Π registers no one-sided prerequisite footprint, because between equivalent skills there is none.

5Real-data demonstrations

ECPE — what this shows

The grammar section of an English proficiency examination: 28 items, n = 2,922 examinees, three attributes (morphosyntactic, cohesive, lexical). The retained edges form a clean three-attribute chain in the recovered dominance order: lexical (skill3) sits at the top, above morphosyntactic (skill1), which sits above cohesive (skill2).

How to read it: an arrow means the source attribute acts as a ceiling on the target — progress on the target stays at or below what the source grants. Walk the ladder pair by pair in the inspector: permutation p first, then Δ for the direction, then the Π pair for per-direction strength.

Fraction subtraction — what this shows

536 examinees, eight attributes of fraction subtraction. Here the raw edge set contains directed cycles, and five attributes condense into one merged node. Both are shown as-is: they are expected behavior of a pairwise index when attributes share underlying ability, not something the display cleans up.

This is where the honest boundary of the method lives. What the scan recovers is a dominance preorder — which attributes act as ceilings on which others — and not a map of direct teaching prerequisites. Skills that lean on the same underlying ability can dominate each other mutually (a cycle, drawn as a merged node), and a skill can sit above another it was never taught before. If the picture disagrees with an expert-specified hierarchy, that is a difference between two concepts, not by itself an error in either.

6Under the hood & origin

The ceiling mechanism

Π(X→Y) = A1 · A2 — corner emptiness times censoring-aware adherence.

A1 — the empty corner. If X is a prerequisite for Y, people should not outrun their prerequisite: the corner {Y > X} above the diagonal should be empty. A1 measures how empty it is, relative to how full independence would leave it.

A2 = q · ℓ — free below the ceiling, thinning at the top. Points piled onto the ceiling Y ≈ X are read as right-censoring — the prerequisite is binding, which is evidence for the relation, not against it. Below the ceiling, Y should vary as a free component (q); and the censoring should thin out among the highest-X people, whose ceiling is roomiest (ℓ, checked in the top-x stratum). Equivalent skills fail exactly here: everything sits on the ceiling everywhere, so q = ℓ = 0 and Π = 0 even though r ≈ 1.

The product is what lets one number separate the cases: independence is annihilated by A1 alone, equivalence by A2 alone, and only a genuine one-sided ceiling structure keeps both factors alive.

Origin

The anchored scale — 0 as substantive ignorance, 1 as substantive mastery — comes from the Cognitive Trait Model (Choi, 2022, Mathematics, 10(15):2651), developed further in the ALF measurement paper (arXiv:2608.22763). Anchoring is an interpretability requirement of Π — the ratio Y/X and the corner only carry a prerequisite reading when both endpoints are substantive anchors — not a claim about the measurement precision of any scoring model. The prerelation coefficient itself is the subject of a paper in preparation.
