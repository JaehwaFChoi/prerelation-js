Which attributes act as a ceiling on which others?

The prerelation coefficient scores an ordered pair of traits reported on a common anchored scale: the corner above the diagonal has to be empty, and what sits below the ceiling has to stay free. Every number on this page was computed in the browser build of this package.

Pi(X → Y) = A1 · A2,   A2 = q · ell,   Delta = Pi(X → Y) − Pi(Y → X)

Pi matrix

Rows are the candidate prerequisite, columns the dependent trait. Select any cell to inspect that pair. A dot marks a pair that survived the false discovery rate control.

Dominance preorder

Attributes that dominate each other are condensed into one node; the diagram is the Hasse diagram of the quotient order over those classes.

Pair inspector

Shaded above the diagonal: the corner {Y > X}, whose emptiness relative to the independence baseline gives A1. Hatched along the diagonal: the ceiling band, points with Y / X at or above 1 − delta. The rule at the right marks the top-x stratum where legitimacy is checked. Trait values are drawn rounded to six decimals; the statistics were computed at full precision.

The scan recovers a dominance preorder over the attributes — which attributes act as ceilings on which others — not a direct-prerequisite DAG. Indirect dominance produces edges of its own, and siblings under a common ceiling can be linked to each other even though neither is a prerequisite for the other. Cycles and merged nodes are expected behaviour of a pairwise index. A disagreement between the recovered order and an expert-specified prerequisite graph is a difference between two concepts, not by itself an error in either.
